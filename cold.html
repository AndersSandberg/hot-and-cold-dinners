UK Food Ingredients Under Abrupt Sunlight Reduction

What is left on the British plate when the sun goes out, across four severities of catastrophe

An abrupt sunlight reduction scenario is not one event but a family of them: smoke from mass fires after nuclear war, sulfate from a very large eruption, dust from an impact. The published severity ladder comes from six nuclear-war simulations, and the tiers below group them. They are not equivalent hazards, and a soot mass cannot be translated into an equivalent mass of volcanic sulfate.

Two things determine what is eaten. The first is what will grow. The second, and usually the stronger, is whether the country still has the seed, fuel, mills, ports, cold stores and political nerve to turn a crop into a meal. Every entry below assumes an unprepared and fragmented response; where preparation and continued trade would change the answer, it says so.

What the table adds up to

The fat gap

Rapeseed is the only cooking fat Britain can produce at moderate and severe tiers. Olive, sunflower and butter all vanish or become scarce, and the modelled diets flag essential fatty acids as dangerously low. This is not only a nutritional problem. Fat carries flavour, enables browning and provides satiety, so a diet of potatoes, seaweed and protein powder is both marginal and joyless — and people abandon joyless diets.

The flavour collapse

At moderate severity the British spice rack reduces to salt, mustard, horseradish, wild garlic, vinegar, hardy perennial herbs and seaweed. Tomatoes and citrus go, taking the entire acidity system with them, and vinegar has to do that job alone. The recipe problem is not caloric but hedonic: making food from a radically narrowed palette that people will actually choose to eat.

Fermentation is the master technology

Vinegar for preservation and acidity, sauerkraut for vitamin C, malt for calories and morale, yeast extract for B vitamins and umami, seaweed and microbial pastes as condiments. All of it works without refrigeration and most of it works without much energy. Every pre-industrial food culture arrived here independently, which is the strongest evidence that it is the right answer.

Vitamin D is the defining deficiency

It is severely short in every modelled diet, and there is no sunlight to make up the difference through the skin. Current global supplement production covers a small fraction of what would be needed. UV-treated mushrooms and oily fish are the only food-based solutions, which makes mushroom cultivation a medical intervention that happens to taste of something, and gives the British fishing fleet an importance it has not had since the war.

The preservation technologies outlive the refrigeration that replaced them

Fresh milk fails before cheese. Fresh fish fails before the kipper. The slaughter glut feeds people or rots depending on salt, not electricity. Almost every technique the twentieth century made optional becomes load-bearing again, and Britain still has all of them written down.

On Australia. In cooperative modelling, Australia emerges as the one large wheat exporter still functioning, with spring wheat yields improving under moderate cooling. That result comes from a model that explicitly excludes export bans. Since export bans are the instinctive response of a frightened government, the Australian lifeline exists only in the version of the future where nobody panics.

On the numbers. The per-day figures quoted for potatoes, seaweed, spirulina and protein powders describe modelled diet compositions from a global optimisation. They are not statements about what British fields produce. At 150 Tg, modelled UK conventional food output falls by 96–99%, and the diet is assembled from stores, industry and imports rather than from farms.
